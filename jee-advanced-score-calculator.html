Marking Mechanics

JEE Advanced Marking Structure & Partial Negative Rules

Detailed analysis of single correct, multi-choice partial marking, and numerical formats.

Unlike JEE Main, the total marks and question distributions in JEE Advanced are dynamic and announced only on exam day. Standard paper editions usually feature 180 marks in Paper 1 and 180 marks in Paper 2 (360 total aggregate). Both papers are compulsory.

Question Format Correct Response Partial Marking Incorrect Response Unattempted
Single Choice MCQs +3 Marks None -1 Mark 0 Marks
One or More Correct (Multi-Choice) +4 Marks (All correct marked) +3, +2, or +1 for correct partial choices -2 Penalty (Any wrong option) 0 Marks
Numerical Values (Decimals / Integers) +3 or +4 Marks None 0 Marks (or -1 in select formats) 0 Marks
Matching Lists / Matrix Match +3 Marks None -1 Mark 0 Marks
⚠️
The Devastating -2 Multi-Choice Trap
  • In multi-correct questions with options A, B, and C correct:
    • If you mark A, B, C: You receive full +4 marks.
    • If you mark only A and B: You receive partial +2 marks.
    • If you mark A, B, and mistakenly include D: You receive -2 marks!
  • A single misplaced guess turns a potential +2 or +3 into a net -2 penalty (a massive 4-to-5 mark swing per question!).
⚖️
Dual Threshold Rule
  • Scoring 200+ marks total is futile if you fail to score the minimum 10% in any individual subject (Physics, Chemistry, or Math).
  • NTA and JAB strictly disqualify candidates who fail the subject cutoffs from the Common Rank List (CRL) and all category lists.

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to common questions regarding JEE Advanced aggregate marks and IIT admissions.

Total Aggregate Score = (Physics Paper 1 + Paper 2) + (Chemistry Paper 1 + Paper 2) + (Mathematics Paper 1 + Paper 2). Standard papers typically total 360 marks (180 marks in Paper 1 and 180 marks in Paper 2). Both papers are compulsory.
To qualify for the Common Rank List (CRL), a candidate must score a minimum percentage (typically 10% or around 12 marks out of 120) in each of the three subjects individually across Paper 1 and Paper 2 combined, in addition to scoring at least 35% (around 109 to 126 marks out of 360) in aggregate.
If a candidate fails to secure the minimum qualifying marks in even one subject (e.g. Mathematics), they are completely disqualified from all IIT rank lists, regardless of how high their total aggregate score is in the remaining subjects.
In multi-correct questions carrying 4 marks: +4 is awarded if all correct options are selected. Partial marks (+3, +2, or +1) are awarded if only a subset of correct options is chosen without selecting any wrong option. However, if even one incorrect option is selected, a negative penalty of -2 marks is deducted.
For Computer Science at IIT Bombay, IIT Delhi, and IIT Madras (AIR 1 to 150), candidates generally need 285+ marks (around 79% to 85% of full score). For CSE in top 7 IITs (AIR up to 1,000), 220+ marks are typically required.
